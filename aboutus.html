About Us

Cornafulla Micro Reserve: Community-Led Wetland Conservation

Cornafulla Micro Reserve is a community-led organisation working with local landowners, volunteers and the wider community to understand, conserve and communicate the natural, cultural and ecological heritage of Cornafulla Bog and its relationship with the Shannon Callows. We combine wildlife monitoring, research, public engagement and education, supported by collaboration with specialists and public bodies.

Although large areas of the bog have been historically impacted by drainage and peat extraction, Cornafulla Bog still holds significant ecological value. Ecological assessments have identified areas of Active Raised Bog (Annex I habitat 7110, a priority under the EU Habitats Directive), Degraded Raised Bog (7120, capable of natural regeneration), and distinctive Rhynchosporion habitats (7150, important for specific plant communities).

Cornafulla Bog is bordered on three sides by the Middle Shannon Callows Special Protection Area and the River Shannon Callows Special Area of Conservation. Both belong to Natura 2000, the European Union’s network of protected sites for important habitats and species. Although Cornafulla Bog lies outside these designations, its wildlife is closely connected to the protected Callows.

Whooper Swans feed on the Shannon Callows and roost on Cornafulla Bog. Cornafulla Micro Reserve volunteers counted 230 Whooper Swans on 11 January 2026, while trail cameras have recorded Otters on both sides of the Natura 2000 boundary. Whooper Swan is a Special Conservation Interest of the Middle Shannon Callows SPA, and Otter is a qualifying species of the River Shannon Callows SAC. Protecting these species means understanding the habitats they use beyond designated boundaries.

Community, Landowners and Volunteers

Cornafulla Micro Reserve began informally in 2023, initially centred on just 0.8 of an acre of privately owned bog. Through the involvement of neighbouring landowners, volunteers and researchers, the initiative has grown to encompass 168 acres of participating land across Cornafulla Bog and the Shannon Callows. Of this, approximately half (82 acres) lies within the Middle Shannon Callows SPA, the River Shannon Callows SAC, or both.

Landowner cooperation is central to our work. Participating landowners have agreed to biodiversity monitoring and conservation activities on their land while retaining full ownership and control. These agreements have opened up new opportunities for wildlife recording across privately owned land and strengthened relationships between landowners and the community organisation.

Our volunteers bring different skills, experiences and interests to Cornafulla Micro Reserve, including local knowledge of the landscape, farming, wildlife, education, art and technology. We come together through a shared interest in Cornafulla Bog and the Shannon Callows, creating welcoming opportunities for residents, landowners and visitors to experience the landscape and exchange knowledge. Participation does not require prior ecological knowledge or a commitment to becoming a wildlife recorder.

Conservation and Wildlife Monitoring

Our volunteers participate in the International Swan Census, a coordinated survey of swan populations across Ireland and the UK, and BirdWatch Ireland’s Irish Wetland Bird Survey (I-WeBS). Cornafulla Bog is now an I-WeBS subsite, with monthly counts contributing to national waterbird records.

We also record mammals, birds, plants and other wildlife, submitting observations to the National Biodiversity Data Centre. Trail cameras, habitat observations and peat-depth surveys help document wildlife and environmental conditions across the landscape. Volunteers have recorded peat depths exceeding 12 metres in parts of Cornafulla Bog.

Through wildlife monitoring workshops and field activities, we encourage people to develop practical skills, from informal wildlife observation and species identification to contributing records and participating in structured national monitoring programmes.

Though traditional turf cutting remains part of the local culture, there is an increasing recognition within the community of the need for peatland conservation and its role in mitigating climate change. Through voluntary work and collaboration with researchers, conservation organisations and statutory bodies, we are building the skills, resources and partnerships needed to sustain community-led wetland conservation.

Education, Heritage and Community Engagement

Cornafulla Micro Reserve brings local communities together with ecologists, archaeologists, researchers, artists and educators through walks, talks, workshops and public events.

In 2026, we hosted the Bog Bothy, a temporary gathering place overlooking Cornafulla Bog. Its programme of 18 activities brought together local residents and visitors for walks, talks, music, poetry, local history, art and ecology, recording 764 attendances. Local people shared their knowledge, memories and experiences of the landscape alongside invited specialists.

We also hold open meetings on the first Saturday of every month, providing regular opportunities for wildlife observation, field learning and community participation.

Our website serves as a key platform for connecting people with Cornafulla Bog and the Shannon Callows. It highlights the ecology, wildlife, habitats and cultural history of the landscape, providing accessible information for the community, volunteers and supporters. We also share updates on our activities and opportunities for people to take part in wildlife recording, fieldwork and community events.

We believe that by sharing our work and inviting community participation, we can continue to protect the rich biodiversity and heritage of Cornafulla Bog and the Shannon Callows for future generations.
